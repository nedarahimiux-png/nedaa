# Spectrum Business Mobile case study

I led the end-to-end design of Spectrum's first dedicated Business Mobile experience, creating a journey that helped small business customers understand their options, explore plans, and purchase mobile service for their teams.

## 01 — Overview

### Bringing the Business Mobile experience to life

Spectrum Business customers needed a mobile shopping experience built around the way businesses actually buy.

I owned the experience from product strategy through design execution and delivery, partnering with product, UX research, engineering, content, legal, and business stakeholders to bring the first dedicated Business Mobile journey to life.

- **Industry:** Telecommunications
- **Role:** Product Designer / Strategy
- **Scope:** 0→1 B2B ecommerce experience
- **Team:** Product Owner · UX Research

## 02 — The challenge

### Small businesses were being sent through a residential buying experience.

Before launch, business customers had no dedicated way to learn about or buy Spectrum Mobile.

After logging in, they were routed into the residential buyflow—an experience designed for individual consumers, not companies purchasing mobile service for their teams.

The experience wasn't designed around business needs:

- Business customers have different purchasing needs, including buying multiple lines.
- There was no dedicated Business Mobile journey to explain the offering.
- Customers needed a clearer path from learning about the product to completing a purchase.

**The opportunity:** Create a dedicated experience with the information, pricing clarity, and purchasing tools businesses need to confidently buy mobile service.

**Journey:** Small business customer → Residential buyflow; then Small business customer → Dedicated Business Mobile journey.

## 04 — Key design decisions

### Designing around the realities of business customers

### Decision 1 — Making pricing clear without overwhelming the experience

**The challenge:** Business customers could encounter different pricing depending on their signup date and network provider. We needed to communicate pricing details without making the experience feel cluttered.

**My decision:** Rather than repeat disclaimers throughout the experience, I advocated for a persistent sign-in ribbon that kept important pricing information visible.

**Why it mattered:** It created a consistent place for customers to reference important information without interrupting the shopping journey.

**Image:** `PricingRibbon.png` — Spectrum Business Mobile screen showing a persistent sign-in ribbon with current offer and promotion messaging.  
**Caption:** A persistent ribbon keeps offer guidance visible within the shopping journey.

### Decision 2 — Making multi-line savings visible upfront

**The insight:** Business customers often purchase multiple lines for their teams. Showing the value of purchasing multiple lines was important to making the experience relevant to how businesses shop.

**My decision:** I designed a volume-discount experience on the homepage that surfaced potential savings directly, rather than making customers discover the value later in the journey.

**Why it mattered:** Customers could understand the benefit of purchasing multiple lines earlier in their decision-making process.

**Image:** `Volume Discount on the Homepage.png` — Spectrum Business Mobile homepage showing multi-line plan pricing and volume savings.  
**Caption:** The homepage surfaces potential savings for customers purchasing multiple lines.

### Decision 3 — Simplifying shipping for businesses with multiple locations

**The challenge:** Business customers may need devices shipped to multiple addresses. Checkout also had to work within backend address-validation constraints. Once validated, an address could not be edited.

**My decision:** I designed a multi-address shipping modal that let customers reuse previously validated addresses.

**Why it mattered:** This supported a real business purchasing need while working within the technical constraints of the existing system.

**Image:** `Multi-Shipping Addresses.png` — Spectrum Business Mobile checkout modal for selecting multiple previously validated shipping addresses, shown in its checkout context.  
**Caption:** The shipping modal lets business buyers reuse validated addresses for multiple destinations.

## 06 — What's next

### Turning funnel insights into the next round of improvements

After launch, I used funnel data, research, and competitive benchmarks to identify opportunities to improve the experience further. I presented these recommendations to director-level leadership and added them to the product roadmap. They are proposed improvements, not shipped features.

### Phase 1 — Give customers a clearer sense of progress

**Opportunity:** Introduce a checkout progress indicator.  
**Closed tile description:** Help customers understand where they are in the purchasing journey and what comes next.

The goal was to reduce uncertainty, create structure, and help customers understand where they were in the purchasing process. Comparable B2B research suggested a potential 3–8% lift in completed transactions after adding a four-step progress bar. The cited test ran for 55 days with an estimated 10,000–15,000 visitors per variant. Spectrum UX research supported the rationale around reducing uncertainty and building momentum.

### Phase 2 — Make product discovery easier

**Opportunity:** Explore a more effective product-listing experience.  
**Closed tile description:** Make product specifications and comparisons easier for business customers to scan.

Baymard telecommunications benchmark research highlighted product-list and filtering issues. For products with detailed specifications, a list view could make comparisons easier to scan. I explored how a list-based layout could make product specifications easier to compare and identified the bottom of the page as an opportunity for additional marketing content.

Baymard research cited a potential **4× increase in leads** with effective filtering and comparison tools—not a Spectrum outcome. Baymard also found poor-to-mediocre product-list UX on 58% of desktop and 78% of mobile ecommerce sites.

**Image:** `ProductDiscovery.png` — Proposed Spectrum product-list design showing mobile devices and comparison details.

### Phase 3 — Build confidence before customers purchase

**Opportunity:** Introduce dedicated Business Mobile learn pages.  
**Closed tile description:** Give customers the information they need to understand the offering before entering the purchase journey.

The goal was to help customers understand the offering, reduce uncertainty, and feel more confident moving into the purchase journey. Proposed content included:

- **Minimize Your Risk**
- **What You Need to Know to Switch**
- **Support Spectrum Offers**

These pages would give customers more information before asking them to make a purchasing decision.

## 07 — Reflection

### Designing for ambiguity meant balancing what customers needed with what the business could deliver

Building a new experience from the ground up required more than designing screens. It meant navigating incomplete requirements, aligning stakeholders, and finding solutions that balanced customer needs, business goals, and technical constraints.

One thing I'd approach differently next time is bringing UX Research into the process earlier and prioritizing educational content sooner. Dedicated learn pages could have helped customers understand the offering and build confidence before entering the purchase journey.

**Closing thought:** This project reinforced the importance of defining the experience, not just designing it—especially when creating something new within an established product ecosystem.
